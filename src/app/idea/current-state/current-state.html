Current state of research

The existing work in this area falls into four directions. For each of them, the table below shows what is already known and what remains open for my research. The methods themselves are described in the Main alternatives tab.

DirectionWhat is knownWhat is open
RAG vs GraphRAG Whether a graph helps depends on the dataset and on the type of question. Studies compare whole systems, so they measure the total difference between them, but they cannot show how much of it comes from the graph links themselves.
How found facts are presented The same facts can lead to a different answer depending on whether they are given as text or as a graph, and this effect varies with the model and the data. When facts have to be combined, the order of the paragraphs can matter as well. It is still unclear how much of the effect comes from format and order and how much comes from the graph.
Multi-hop retrieval QASA walks through a Neo4j graph and follows the links that fit the question. IRCoT reaches the same facts without a graph, because it searches, reasons about what it has found and then searches again. It is not yet clear how much the graph links add beyond what one gets by simply searching again.
Selective graph indexing KET-RAG builds the graph for a fixed share of the pieces of text, and it picks them by how central they are in a similarity graph. The quality of such a graph can be checked by whether it actually contains the chains of facts that the answers need. It is not known whether picking pieces by how useful they turned out to be still works when new questions are asked.
