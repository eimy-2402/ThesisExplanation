Basic RAG

Basic RAG consists of five steps. The first two steps, together called indexing, are done once in advance, while steps 3 to 5 are repeated for every question.

  1. Split into chunks. The documents are cut into short chunks. The study in the last section uses chunks of about 256 tokens, where a token is a word or a piece of a word. In my pilot, each chunk was one Wikipedia paragraph.
  2. Index. Each chunk is stored twice, once in a keyword index and once as an embedding in a vector index, so that it can be found both by its words and by its meaning.
  3. Hybrid search. When a question arrives, both indexes are searched, the two result lists are merged, and a reranker puts the most relevant chunks on top. The k best chunks are kept.
  4. Pass to the model. The question and the top-k chunks are put together into one prompt for the language model.
  5. Generate. The model writes the answer based on those chunks.

The search in step 3 combines two different searches. The keyword search uses BM25, short for Best Matching 25, which is a classic scoring formula. It gives a chunk a higher score when the chunk contains more of the question’s words, and especially when those words are rare. The vector search compares embeddings instead, so it also finds chunks that express the same idea in other words.

After that, a reranker model reads the question together with each candidate chunk and estimates how likely it is that the chunk is relevant, and the candidates are sorted by this estimate. A well-known early setup of this kind used BERT to rerank passages that BM25 had found.
