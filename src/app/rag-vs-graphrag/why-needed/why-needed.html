Why we need it

A language model only knows what it saw during training. RAG gives it the relevant text at the moment the question is asked, and this helps in four ways.

  • Knowledge goes stale. A model stops learning when its training ends, so it knows nothing that was written afterwards. RAG can search documents that are newer than the model, which keeps its answers current.
  • The model has never seen private files. A company’s internal documents were never part of the training data, so the model cannot know what they say. RAG passes the model the relevant parts of these documents whenever a question comes in.
  • No retraining is needed. Because the knowledge lives in the documents rather than in the model, adding or correcting a fact only means updating the documents, while the model itself stays the same.
  • Answers can show their sources. Since the answer is written from known chunks, the system can show which documents it used. Without the search step, a model cannot reliably tell where a fact came from.

However, RAG does not guarantee correct answers. If the search misses the paragraph that contains the answer, the model never sees it and has to answer without it.
