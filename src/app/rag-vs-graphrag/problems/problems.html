Where basic RAG breaks

The paragraph that the answer needs can be lost at any step of this process. The table below describes three common ways in which this happens.

ProblemWhat happens
The needed paragraph does not look like the question Consider the question “Where was the director of the film Inception born?” The paragraph that says where the director was born is about the director as a person and never mentions the film, so it has little in common with the question. It only becomes clear what to search for once a first search has revealed who the director is.
Chunk boundaries Cutting a document into chunks can separate a sentence from its context. For example, a chunk saying that revenue grew by 3% may not say which company it refers to.
Limited context Only k chunks reach the model, so anything ranked lower is lost. Moreover, models make worse use of the middle of a long input than of its beginning or end, so even a chunk that reaches the model may have little effect on the answer.

My pilot shows how early in the process this can go wrong. Out of the 488 paragraphs that were needed, 112 were not even among the 30 candidates that reached the reranker, so no later step could bring them back.
